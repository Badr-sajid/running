# D1 — Apple Watch / HealthKit data for running — round 1 digest

Accessed: 2026-11-25 for all sources. Budget used: 20 tool calls (~13 distinct sources touched; several read only via search-result summaries, which I flag as "snippet-level").
Note on method: claims marked "snippet-level" were seen in search-engine summaries of the named page, not in a full-page fetch. Re-verify these before citing them as strong evidence.

## (a) Findings

### Device features / HealthKit

1. **claim:** HealthKit exposes the running form metrics as quantity types: `runningPower` ("rate of work required for the runner to maintain their speed"), `runningGroundContactTime`, `runningSpeed`, `runningStrideLength`, `runningVerticalOscillation`, all introduced with iOS 16 / watchOS 9. There is **no dedicated running-cadence quantity type**. Cadence has to be derived, e.g. from stepCount over time or from the FIT exported by a third-party app.
   source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/runningpower , https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/runninggroundcontacttime , https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708208 | publisher: Apple Developer | pub_date: 2022-06 (iOS 16 types; forum thread 2022) | confidence: medium (snippet-level; the "no cadence type" point comes from a 2022 forum post, so a newer type may exist, needs checking) | class: device-feature
2. **claim:** Apple's support page defines ground contact time as the time the foot is on the ground, in ms. Running power is the output of the work done when running, in watts. Both are available while running only: no power and no GCT during walking, and no cycling power. Running form metrics need Series 6 or later (power: Series 4+ at the watchOS 9 launch, per the5krunner).
   source: https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/apd1f24d4d35/watchos ; https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/add-running-form-metrics-apple-watch/ | publisher: Apple Support; MacRumors (press) | pub_date: 2025 (current guide, exact date unknown) | confidence: medium (snippet-level) | class: device-feature
3. **claim:** Training Load (watchOS 11+) compares the intensity × duration of workouts over the **last 7 days with the previous 28 days**. It classifies the result on a scale from "well below" to "well above" and can be filtered by workout type. The Activity app shows it under a "Workload" button. It links to overnight Vitals. The page does **not** document the effort scale, its inputs (HR, age, weight) or which workouts get an automatic effort score. Those details are on a separate "Rate your effort" page that I did not read.
   source: https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/track-your-training-load-apde4c07a6cf/watchos | publisher: Apple Support | pub_date: 2025/2026 (current guide) | confidence: high (fetched) | class: device-feature
4. **claim:** Sleep Score (watchOS 26 / iOS 26, Sept 2025) combines **Duration, Bedtime (consistency) and Interruptions**. Compatible with Series 6+, SE 2+ and all Ultras. watchOS/iOS 26.2 (Dec 2025) recalibrated the bands, which were judged too lenient: the old bands were Very Low 0-29 / Low 30-49 / OK 50-69 / High 70-89, and "Excellent" was renamed "Very High". Users report lower scores after 26.2. So **a score's meaning changes across OS versions**, and historical comparisons break at the 26.2 boundary.
   source: https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/10/watchos-26-sleep-score/ ; https://www.macrumors.com/2025/11/04/watchos-26-2-updates-sleep-score-ranges/ ; https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256217192 | publisher: MacRumors (press, secondary); Apple Community | pub_date: 2025-09-10 ; 2025-11-04 | confidence: medium (no Apple Support page found) | class: device-feature
5. **claim:** Sleep stages: Awake / Core / Deep / REM since watchOS 9. Apple published an updated validation white paper, "Estimating Sleep Stages from Apple Watch", in Oct 2025, with design and validation on separate subjects. I could not read its numbers (PDF > 10 MB).
   source: https://www.apple.com/kr/health/pdf/Estimating_Sleep_Stages_from_Apple_Watch_Oct_2025.pdf | publisher: Apple (vendor white paper) | pub_date: 2025-10 | confidence: medium (existence confirmed, content not read) | class: device-feature

### Accuracy studies

6. **claim:** VO2max ("Cardio Fitness"), Apple Watch Series 7, n=19 (Caserman et al.): reasonable overall agreement (Bland-Altman) but **poor reliability**. The watch overestimates for low-fitness subjects and underestimates for high-fitness subjects. The authors question the bias and representativeness of Apple's own validation.
   source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11325102 (JMIR Biomed Eng 2024;9:e59459) | publisher: JMIR Biomedical Engineering (peer-reviewed) | pub_date: 2024-08 | confidence: medium (snippet-level) | class: accuracy-study
7. **claim:** VO2max, Lambe et al., n=30, data collected Dec 2023 to Jul 2024: the watch **underestimates by about 6 mL/kg/min on average, MAPE ≈13%**. Not ready for clinical use, possibly useful as a practical alternative to submaximal tests. For a runner, the absolute value is unreliable and only the trend may be usable.
   source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40373042/ (PLoS One 2025, 10.1371/journal.pone.0323741) | publisher: PLoS One (peer-reviewed) | pub_date: 2025-05 | confidence: medium (snippet-level; watch model not confirmed) | class: accuracy-study
8. **claim:** Sleep, Apple Watch **Series 8** vs PSG (Mass General Brigham, n=35 healthy adults, inpatient): sleep duration is similar to PSG, and agreement on stages is "moderate to substantial". Apple's per-stage sensitivity ranges from **50.5% to 86.1%**.
   source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39460013/ (PMC11511193) | publisher: peer-reviewed (Sensors 2024, to be confirmed) | pub_date: 2024 | confidence: medium (snippet-level) | class: accuracy-study
9. **claim:** Sleep, Apple Watch **Series 6** (Sensors 2022, 6 devices): sleep/wake agreement 88% but **κ=0.30**. 4-state staging agreement 53%, **κ=0.20**. Conclusion: every device needs to improve on stages. This is older software (pre-watchOS 9 staging?), so treat it as a weak indicator of current accuracy.
   source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36016077/ (PMC9412437) | publisher: MDPI Sensors (peer-reviewed) | pub_date: 2022-08 | confidence: medium | class: accuracy-study
10. **claim:** A 2025 study in Sleep Advances validated 6 devices including the Apple Watch Series 8 (n=62) for staging against PSG. I did not retrieve the Apple-specific results.
    source: https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/6/2/zpaf021/8090472 ; PMC12038347 | publisher: Sleep Advances (OUP, peer-reviewed) | pub_date: 2025 | confidence: low (content not read) | class: accuracy-study
11. **claim:** Optical HR during running: validity **drops as intensity rises** (walking → running). Observations (Series 6, press testing) show **lag against a chest strap during intervals**, when HR rises and falls quickly. Apple Watch 4 is within the ANSI/CTA threshold (<10% MAPE) on a treadmill at steady pace. I found **no peer-reviewed study of the Apple Watch on structured intervals**.
    source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6732081 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36829748/ ; https://coachweb.com/gear/fitness-trackers/are-apple-and-garmin-heart-rate-monitors-accurate-this-study-says-yes | publisher: peer-reviewed (PMC) + press (Coach) | pub_date: 2019–2023 | confidence: medium for the trend, low for the interval lag (press source) | class: accuracy-study
12. **claim:** Apple running power: **no peer-reviewed validation against Stryd or a force plate** found. The only comparisons are single-athlete blog tests (2022): every brand gives different values (no standard), and Apple's trace showed a slow ramp-up on short efforts and anomalies at walking pace and in cadence detection. For context, Stryd itself underestimates power in the lab (Imbach 2020, n=6) but tracks VO2/speed well (van Rassel 2023).
    source: https://the5krunner.com/2022/08/06/running-power-comparisons-more-comparisons-with-apple-garmin-stryd-coros-polar/ ; https://dcrainmaker.com/2022/06/running-comparison-garmin.html ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7404478/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10649254/ | publisher: blogs (non-academic); Sensors/MDPI | pub_date: 2022-06/08; 2020; 2023 | confidence: medium (absence of validation); low (Apple-specific behavior, old watchOS 9 beta) | class: accuracy-study
13. **claim:** Apple Watch HRV is stored as **SDNN** (derived from PPG), not RMSSD. intervals.icu had to add a separate field for it. SDNN and RMSSD are not interchangeable, so HRV-guided training rules (built mostly on RMSSD, e.g. ln-rMSSD) cannot be applied directly. Validation: Series 6 vs 3-lead ECG (Sensors 2025, n=78, ages 20-75; results not read). A 2018 study shows **gaps (beats not detected) under stress**. Wrist PRV is not as well validated as finger PPG. No Apple-published SDNN validation found.
    source: https://forum.intervals.icu/t/healthfit-sync-for-activities-and-health-metrics/2718?page=4 ; https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/8/2380 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6111985/ | publisher: intervals.icu forum (David, intervals developer); MDPI Sensors; Sensors 2018 | pub_date: 2021-03 ; 2025-04 ; 2018 | confidence: high (SDNN); medium (validity) | class: accuracy-study

### Integration HealthFit → intervals.icu

14. **claim:** HealthFit "mirrors Apple Health": Health is its only source of truth (HealthFit developer, "slizeray"/Stéphane). Wellness data synced to intervals.icu seen on the forum: HRV (SDNN), sleep, weight, SpO2, body fat, steps (later version). Automatic sync has to be enabled in HealthFit and runs "shortly after the morning", not in real time. Reported issues: weight not persisting (2021, unresolved), and HRV averaged across the day instead of the waking/nightly measurement (requested, no fix seen). A sleep-duration bug was attributed to a Garmin source, not to the Apple Watch.
    source: https://forum.intervals.icu/t/healthfit-sync-for-activities-and-health-metrics/2718?page=4 | publisher: intervals.icu forum | pub_date: 2021-03 to 2022-01 (old) | confidence: medium (old threads, behavior may have changed) | class: integration
15. **claim:** HealthFit **imports planned workouts from intervals.icu** (beta 2025-09-16, GA 2025-10-10, per the developer). Since 2026-03-23 the workout planner **requires the "Fanatical Supporter" plan**. A user reported planned workouts not showing on the Apple Watch (2025-10-13, unresolved). The thread **does not specify** the mechanism (WorkoutKit / scheduled workouts in the native Workout app) or the supported targets (pace/HR/power).
    source: https://forum.intervals.icu/t/import-planned-workouts-from-intervals-icu-into-apple-health/112136 ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/healthfit/id1202650514 | publisher: intervals.icu forum (HealthFit developer); App Store | pub_date: 2025-09 to 2026-03 | confidence: high (existence + paywall); low (mechanism) | class: integration
16. **claim:** Alternatives in the ecosystem: **IntervalsWellnessSync** (third-party app) syncs sleep duration, sleep score, HRV and RHR overnight, taking HRV/RHR **only from sleep sessions**. Its vendor says Apple Watch has no native sync with intervals.icu, unlike Garmin and WHOOP. **Watchletic** and **WorkOutdoors** import structured workouts from intervals.icu onto the watch. There is also an "Intervals.icu Companion" app on the App Store.
    source: https://forum.intervals.icu/t/intervalswellnesssync-simple-apple-watch-health-data-sync-to-intervals-icu/125843 ; https://www.watchletic.com/intervalsicu ; https://apps.apple.com/app/id6739638454 | publisher: forum / vendors (marketing register) | pub_date: 2025-2026 | confidence: low-medium (vendor claims) | class: integration

## (b) Leads worth chasing

- **"Rate your effort" (Apple Support)**: effort scale 1-10, automatic estimate (from HR + GPS/power?) for running vs manual entry, and whether effort ends up in HealthKit (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.workoutEffortScore` / `estimatedWorkoutEffortScore`, iOS 18). Undocumented types according to an Apple forum. Does HealthFit export them to intervals?
- **Apple Oct 2025 sleep white paper**: get the per-stage numbers (smaller PDF on apple.com/healthcare/docs or a US version). Also the white papers "Estimating VO2max" / "Using Apple Watch to Estimate Cardio Fitness" (not searched, budget ran out).
- **FIT contents exported by HealthFit**: does it include power, GCT, vertical oscillation, stride length, laps/segments, dual-frequency GPS and HR at 1 Hz or lower? Apple records HR at a variable interval during workouts. Check healthfitapp.com directly (not surfaced by search).
- **HR sampling cadence in HealthKit during workouts** (≈ every 1-5 s?), and its effect on TRIMP / HR-based load in intervals.icu.
- **Planned-workout mechanism** HealthFit → Watch: WorkoutKit `ScheduledWorkoutPlan`? Supported targets? Ask the developer or read the release notes.
- Possible contradiction: HealthFit sends daily-averaged SDNN (forum 2021) while IntervalsWellnessSync takes sleep-only HRV. Which SDNN sample (nightly vs daytime) feeds intervals determines how valid any readiness metric is.
- Sleep Advances 2025 (Series 8, n=62) and Sensors 2025 HRV (Series 6, n=78): read the full results.
- Vitals app (watchOS 11): which metrics (HR, respiratory rate, wrist temperature, SpO2, sleep duration) and their "outlier" thresholds. Not searched.
- Dual-frequency GPS (Ultra / Series 10?) accuracy for distance/pace: no study searched.
