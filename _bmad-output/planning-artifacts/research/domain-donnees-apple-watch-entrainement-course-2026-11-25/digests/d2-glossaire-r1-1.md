| VO2max | The maximal rate of O2 uptake, in mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹. Lab criterion is a VO2 plateau on a graded test. Watches estimate it from the HR–speed relationship (Apple "Cardio Fitness"). Typical values are ~35–50 for recreational adults and 70–85 for elite male distance runners. | definition |
| VMA / MAS, vVO2max | The lowest speed that elicits VO2max (Billat). Field tests include the Léger/UMTT, VAM-Eval and the 5-6-min test, and Cooper 12-min distance gives a rough estimate. It can be held for ~4–8 min (tlim). Many French plans express paces as %VMA. | definition |
| LT1 / LT2, VT1 / VT2 | LT1 is the first sustained lactate rise above baseline (~1.5–2 mM) and maps approximately to VT1. LT2 / "anaerobic threshold" is often fixed at 4 mM or defined as Dmax, and maps approximately to VT2 / RCP. The terminology is inconsistent across studies (see Jamnick). | definition |
| Critical speed and D′ | CS is the asymptote of the speed–duration hyperbola. D′ is the finite distance that can be covered above CS. Estimate CS from 2–4 maximal efforts lasting ~2–15 min, using the linear distance–time model d = CS·t + D′. | formula |
| Running economy | The O2 cost (mL·kg⁻¹·km⁻¹) or energy cost at a given submaximal speed. It is lab-only and varies by ~±15–30% between runners. | definition |
| HRR / Karvonen | Target HR = HRrest + %HRR × (HRmax − HRrest). | formula |
| 5/7-zone models | The 5-zone version is Friel (HR zones based on LTHR, e.g. Z2 81–89% LTHR, unverified). The 7-zone version is Coggan (power zones based on FTP). intervals.icu offers both, user-configurable. | definition |
| Daniels E/M/T/I/R and VDOT | VDOT is a "pseudo-VO2max" derived from a race result using the Daniels–Gilbert equations. The paces are: E at ~59–74% vVO2max; M at marathon pace; T ≈ 1 h race pace (~83–88%); I ≈ vVO2max (3–5 min reps); R faster than I, for economy. | formula |
| RPE (Borg CR10) | A 0–10 category-ratio scale. The original Borg scale runs 6–20. | definition |
| TRIMP: Banister / Edwards / Lucia | Banister: duration × ΔHRratio × 0.64e^(1.92·ΔHRratio), with 1.67 in place of 1.92 for women. Edwards: Σ(min in 5 HR zones × 1–5). Lucia: time in the 3 VT-based zones × 1/2/3. | formula |
| Banister impulse–response | Performance = k1·fitness − k2·fatigue, with τ1 ≈ 42 d and τ2 ≈ 7 d. This is the conceptual origin of CTL/ATL. | formula |
| Resting HR | Best measured on waking or overnight. A rise of more than ~5 bpm above baseline can flag fatigue or illness. | guidance |
| SDNN vs RMSSD | SDNN reflects total variability and needs longer recordings. RMSSD is vagal and stable in short windows (≥ ~1–5 min). Apple Watch reports SDNN (to verify in the wearables dimension). | definition |
| Cardiac drift | HR rises over time at a constant pace (heat, dehydration, fatigue). It is the physiology behind decoupling. | definition |
| Periodization | Linear/traditional: volume then intensity, in macro/meso/microcycles. Block: concentrated mesocycles. Reverse: intensity first, then volume. | definition |
| Taper | Cut volume ~40–60% over ~2 weeks while keeping intensity and frequency (Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis). | guidance |
| Supercompensation | Stress, fatigue, recovery, then a rise above baseline. The model is heuristic and simplified. | definition |
| Session types | Easy: Z1, conversational. Long: the longest weekly run, mostly Z1. Tempo: sustained ~T pace, 20–40 min. Interval: repeated reps at I/vVO2max with recovery. Strides: 15–30 s accelerations to about mile pace, with full recovery. Fartlek: unstructured speed play. Progression: a run that finishes faster than it starts. | definition |

## (b) Leads worth chasing

1. **intervals.icu official docs vs forum.** Every intervals.icu definition here comes from the forum. Find the official docs page for Load, HRSS, pace load and the Form zone bounds, and check whether the "today vs yesterday" Form convention still holds in 2026. The app must use the same convention or its numbers will drift from intervals.icu's.
2. **EF direction.** The fetched summary of the TrainingPeaks glossary said a "drop" in EF indicates improvement. Physically, EF = speed / HR, so a *rise* means improvement. Re-read the source verbatim.
3. **MLSS vs CS vs LT2 vs "threshold pace".** Jones 2019 says they are not interchangeable: MLSS < CP. TrainingPeaks' "threshold" is a ~1 h effort. Decide which anchor the app uses and name it explicitly.
4. **% anchors.** Jamnick 2020 says %HRmax and %VO2max don't reliably place athletes in a domain. That favours threshold-anchored zones (LTHR, CS or threshold pace) over %HRmax or 220-age in the app.
5. **ACWR.** It is contested. Don't use it as an injury predictor. If shown at all, present ramp rate and CTL deltas as descriptive only.
6. **HRV index mismatch.** Apple Watch exports SDNN, but the evidence base uses lnRMSSD. Check what intervals.icu ingests from Apple Health. The search summary also attributed "spectral more sensitive than time-domain" to Buchheit 2014, which looks like the opposite of his usual position, so verify it.
7. **Polarized vs pyramidal.** Recent meta-analyses find little difference, possibly with pyramidal suiting recreational runners. This matters for the app's default plan template.
8. **The 2025 IPD network meta-analysis.** Its exact citation and URL were not captured.
