**Two-source status for the progression rule.** There is only one primary cohort for the session-spike threshold (2.1, with the authors' own blog). Nobody has independently replicated the session-level 10% threshold, so call it "best current evidence, single cohort".

### Implications for the generator and the disruption handler

These are a synthesis, not a source.

- **Hard constraint:** cap each session's distance at ≤ 1.10 × the longest run in the trailing 30 days. Small increments are preferred, and an exception needs explicit user override plus a warning (2.1, 2.3). Do not rely on a weekly 10% rule as the safety mechanism (2.2, 2.4). A weekly ceiling is a secondary guard only (2.5).
- **Novice, or previous-injury flag:** these runners carry higher baseline risk (1.1, 3.1).
- **Illness:** fever or any below-neck or systemic symptom means cancel, then at least 5 days off, then a staged return with ≥48 h per stage and no normal training before about day 10. Above-neck only means downgrade to easy or cancel, with normal training no earlier than about day 5. Chest pain, palpitations or exertional breathlessness mean stop and refer to a clinician (4.1–4.4).
- **Injury:** pain on a 0–10 scale is the progression gate (≤5/10 in the tendinopathy model, verified). Bone-stress or pain-on-walking signals mean stop running and refer. Return through walk-run, progressing volume before intensity (5.1, 5.2).
- **After a break:** regenerate the plan with reduced volume and intensity. Losses are a few percent of VO2max per 2–4 weeks off (6.1). A specific percentage-reduction table, such as Daniels', was not verified (see below).
- **REDs flags:** amenorrhoea, a history of stress fractures, or unexplained declining performance with fatigue mean a non-diagnostic flag and a recommendation to see a physician (7.1, 7.2).

## (b) Leads worth chasing

- Full text of Frandsen et al. 2025 BJSM (PMC12421110): exact definitions, whether the effect holds for novices vs experienced runners, how the "longest run in 30 days" is computed after a break (this matters for the disruption handler), and pace or intensity spikes.
- Nielsen RO et al. BJSM 2020 consensus "Training load and structure-specific load: applications for sport injury causality" and the 2019 "Ten-percent rule" commentary. Both explain why weekly ratios fail.
- Silbernagel 2007 primary text: verify the next-morning-pain and 24 h criteria. Also Thomeé 1997 pain-monitoring model.
- The IOC acute respiratory illness review (Snyders, Schwellnus et al., BJSM 2022 / Sports Med) that underlies claim 4.3. Also the BJSM 2025 "return to sport after acute respiratory illness" consensus, if one exists.
- REDs CAT2 primary text (Stellingwerff et al., BJSM 2023): exact primary and secondary indicators. Also the 2023 female athlete bone stress injury consensus (Hoenig, Warden et al.).
- Daniels' Running Formula: rules for reducing VDOT after a break (for example, by days off and cross-training). I could not reach the primary book.
- Cadence retraining (Heiderscheit 2011; Willy 2016) for patellofemoral pain, tibial stress and knee load.
- Low weekly frequency, BMI and sex as risk factors: van Gent 2007 BJSM, and Hulme 2017 Sports Med "Risk and protective factors for middle- and long-distance running-related injury".

## (c) Looked for, not found (this run)

- I could not read the full Garmin-RUNSAFE paper (PMC blocked). Spike definitions came from the authors' BJSM blog.
- I found no primary source for the "neck check" rule, only statements that it lacks scientific backing.
- I could not verify a fever-specific numeric rule (for example, "≥38 °C means no training until 24–48 h afebrile"). Only "no return before fever resolves" was found.
- The next-morning criterion in the Silbernagel model was not confirmed.
- Daniels' VDOT reduction rules after a break were not searched (budget) and are not verified.
- I found no detraining data specific to recreational runners. Mujika & Padilla figures came from secondary sources.
- I found no evidence base retrieved for running surface or shoes as risk factors, BMI, sex, or weekly frequency (budget). Van Gent 2007 was not retrieved.
- I found no running-specific meta-analysis showing that strength training prevents RRI. Running RCTs are mixed.
